# Measurement Quality

Use for multi-item scores, reliability, adapted measures, or comparisons across groups/time. Request item wording/coding, scoring rules, item-level model output and relevant sample/level details when the manuscript is insufficient.

- Match the construct structure, scoring rule and reliability estimand. Distinguish internal consistency (alpha/omega), temporal stability and interrater reliability/agreement. For ESM, inspect whether reliability is appropriate to the within-person or between-person score being interpreted.
- A high alpha neither establishes unidimensionality nor validity. A multidimensional scale is not automatically invalid: examine whether its total score has a defensible measurement model. Use alpha/omega appropriate to that model; omega is not a universal cure. Avoid pass/fail rules based solely on .70 or automatic demands for every reliability index.
- For new or substantially adapted scales, examine content coverage and evidence for the proposed structure. Do not automatically demand EFA before CFA for established measures, or describe same-sample exploration and confirmation as independent validation.
- Examine loadings, factor correlations, residuals and discriminant validity jointly. A better fit than a single-factor model, AVE or HTMT threshold alone does not settle construct distinctness. Do not delete content merely to improve fit or alpha.
- Trigger measurement invariance checks when substantive group/time comparisons require comparable measurement. Determine the needed constraints from the comparison (e.g., loadings for relationships; intercepts/thresholds for latent means), indicator type and identification. Partial invariance needs explicit released constraints and justification; invariance is not obligatory for every single-group survey.

Read [sem-cfa.md](sem-cfa.md) for model estimation. Methodological anchors: [Cortina et al., alpha to omega](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000815); [Rönkkö & Cho, discriminant validity](https://doi.org/10.1177/1094428120968614). These support conditional judgment, not universal cutoffs.
